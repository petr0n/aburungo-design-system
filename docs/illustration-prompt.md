# Chapter illustration — generation prompts

For Gemini image generation. Each block below is **complete and self-contained** —
copy one, paste it, generate. No assembly needed.

Generate **one chapter at a time**. The style block is identical across all five on
purpose; changing a word between images is the main cause of style drift.

Direction set by the author 2026-08-24: Japanese woodcut aesthetic, line art, DS
colours, explicitly not anime. Scenes are the real Book One chapters from
`../aburungo/src/content/chapters/n5.yaml`.

## Ch1 · Greetings & ordering

```
A traditional Japanese woodblock print (mokuhanga) illustration, in the aizuri-e style
where deep indigo carries the line work. The linework must look carved into wood —
irregular weight, slight chatter along the edges, blunt terminations, the character of a
knife rather than a pen tool. Flat areas of colour with no gradients, no shading, no
rendering, no 3D. A very slight registration offset between the line block and the colour
blocks, the way a hand-pulled print misaligns by a fraction of a millimetre. Subtle
laid-paper grain across the whole image.

Composition is clear and confident: one subject, readable silhouettes, generous empty
ground around them. Within those shapes, carved detail is welcome and wanted — hatching,
wood grain, fabric folds, repeated knife marks, the fine incised texture a woodblock print
carries. Detail lives INSIDE the large shapes; it never becomes clutter around them.

The image is displayed large, roughly the width of a phone screen, so fine linework will
be seen. Do not simplify for small sizes.

THE SCENE: a small restaurant counter seen from the customer's side. A split noren curtain
hanging above. One seated customer and one cook behind the counter, the customer inclining
slightly in a greeting. A bowl and chopsticks on the counter. A standing card beside them bearing only abstract
tally marks and small carved dashes — no words, no characters, nothing readable.

The people are ordinary contemporary Japanese adults, observed plainly. Adults with
real faces, carved rather than cartooned — the restraint of a print portrait, not the
blankness of a pictogram and not the expressiveness of a cartoon.

Square format, 1:1.

Avoid entirely: anime, manga, chibi, kawaii, cartoon mascots, large expressive eyes. Avoid
Mount Fuji, cherry blossoms, torii gates, geisha, samurai, dragons, koi, and every other
tourist emblem of Japan. Avoid modern flat-vector corporate illustration and rounded
blobby figures. Avoid gradients, drop shadows, glows, 3D rendering and photorealism. Avoid
any lettering, text, signage or written characters anywhere in the image. Avoid any
decorative border or frame around the image, and any ornamental marks, seals, glyphs or
characters in the corners or margins.

Colour is assigned by PRINT BLOCK, not by observation. Objects are not coloured the colour
they would be in life — a metal pot, a wooden table and a ceramic bowl are all filled with
flat colour drawn from the palette below, chosen for composition rather than realism.

There are no browns, no greys, no beiges, no wood tones and no metal tones anywhere in the
image. Every filled area is one of: deep indigo #1F3A66, verdigris green #4F9C8D, antique
gold #C9A045, or the warm paper ground #FFFDF8, never pure white.

SHADING — permitted, in the two ways a woodblock print actually does it:
- BOKASHI: a gradient hand-wiped across a flat plane — a sky, a water surface, a
  background field, a ground plane. It sits IN the plane and never wraps around an
  object. Hiroshige's skies are the reference.
- CARVED HATCHING: parallel or crossed knife strokes cut into the block, for fabric
  folds, hair, and areas in shadow. It is line, not tone.

NOT permitted: volumetric rendering. No gradient following the curve of an object to make
it read as three-dimensional, no specular highlight, no sheen on metal or ceramic, no
soft-edged cast shadow. A pot is a flat shape with carved line describing it, never a
rendered solid.

PRINT ARTEFACTS — the marks of an actual hand-pulled print, not simulated wear:
- Woodgrain from the block itself printing faintly through the large flat colour areas,
  running in one consistent direction within each area.
- Uneven ink density across a flat field — slightly heavier where the baren pressed
  harder, thinner and flecked with bare paper where the block ran dry.
- Faint circular burnishing swirls from the baren across the broadest colour areas.
- Slight misregistration between the colour blocks and the key line block: a hairline of
  bare paper along one side of a shape, a sliver of colour overlapping the line on the
  other.
- Pigment feathering a fraction of a millimetre into the paper fibres at the edge of each
  colour area, softening the edge without blurring it.
- Occasional small ticks and specks of stray colour just outside a shape's edge, where the
  block picked up ink it should not have.

All of this texture must be IRREGULAR. It is ink meeting paper, not a pattern fill: it does
not repeat, it does not align itself to the shape it sits inside, and its density varies
across a single flat area. A uniform cross-hatch, an even screen tone, or a repeating
weave swatch laid over a garment is wrong — that reads as digital fill. Wood grain is the
right model for how ALL of it should behave: wandering, uneven, indifferent to the edges
of the shape it happens to fall in.

These artefacts come from the PRINTING, not from the subject — they are what the press did
to the paper, distinct from the bokashi and hatching above, which are what the artist
intended.
```

## Fixes for the three failures you will actually hit

Append to the prompt, don't rewrite it.

```
Too soft or too illustrative:
  heavier carved line, fewer colour areas, more empty paper

Drifting anime-adjacent (almost always the faces):
  faces reduced to a few carved strokes, no eyes rendered at all

Colour drifting off palette:
  restate the hex list once more at the very end — image models weight the
  tail of a prompt heavily
```

## Before accepting anything

Downscale to 80×80 and look at it. A chapter thumbnail that turns to mud at that size
fails regardless of how good it looks at full resolution.

## Landing an approved image

Save into `assets/` as `chapter-<n>-<slug>.png`. Wiring follows the crest pipeline in
`src/brand.css` — the pattern that already works, rather than a new one.
