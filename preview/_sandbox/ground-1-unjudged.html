The ground under chart and keyboard — bare vs crest

The question. DESIGN.md "Which surfaces carry the ground", settled 2026-08-16, puts chart and keyboard in the bare row alongside card, drill and summary. The stated reason is that a texture behind a judged answer competes with the maru — kamon are circular and ○ is taken. The chart and the keyboard never judge anything and never draw a maru, so the reason does not reach them. PR #51 proposes moving these two and leaving the rest bare. This page is the artefact that retires that proposal.

What the render settles immediately. Both components are opaque containers — KanaGrid is bg-surface, KanaKeyboard is bg-keyboard-bg. The crest cannot reach either one's contents. Nothing about kana legibility is at stake; the only thing that changes is the gutter around the component. So this is a compositional choice, not a contrast one, and the contrast gate has nothing to say about it either way.

My read — the call is the author's. Take both. On the chart the crest does what it does on the lesson list: gives the card something to sit on, so it lifts without a shadow. That one was never in doubt.

D′ is what changed my mind on the keyboard. Before rendering at true size I expected three competing materials and was ready to argue against it. At 390 × 380 the crest is a narrow textured margin at the sides and a sliver above the slab — it reads as paper the keyboard is resting on, not as a third field fighting for attention. The wide column above is what makes D look risky, and the wide column is not a phone. Judge D on D′.

So the rule can stay simple rather than growing the second clause I was about to propose: a surface carries the crest when it judges nothing. Both rows move, and "introduces no second colour field of its own" turns out to be a condition the render does not support.
